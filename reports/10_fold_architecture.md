# Report 10 — Point 4.2 Fold Architecture

Nine walk-forward folds generated from the fixed calendar rule, each with a 45-day preceding warm-up buffer, plus a non-vacuous test proving the buffer length actually does its job.

**Firewall intact.** This module produces date ranges, bar counts and signal counts. Signal *counts* only — no outcome is read, `simulate` is never imported, and an AST-level test enforces both.

**Holdout sealed.** 2025-01-01 → 2026-07-26 is *defined* here and never *loaded*. Every loader takes `authorised: bool = False` and raises on the default path.

### The warm-up sufficiency test

**45 days vs 90 days: BIT-IDENTICAL on every indicator, every symbol.**

Fold 5 (mid-dataset), 26,400 bars compared per indicator per symbol, `max_abs_diff` exactly `0.0` and zero NaN mismatches throughout:

| indicator | BTCUSDT | ETHUSDT | SOLUSDT |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMA20 (`ema_fast`) | identical | identical | identical |
| EMA50 (`ema_slow`) | identical | identical | identical |
| Donchian-20 upper | identical | identical | identical |
| Donchian-20 lower | identical | identical | identical |
| Session RVOL baseline (`baseline_days=20`) | identical | identical | identical |
| ATR(14) | identical | identical | identical |
| *(RSI — informational only after 3R)* | identical | identical | identical |

Bit-identity was the bar, not approximate equality, and it was met. Over a 45-day buffer (4,320 bars) EMA50's memory of its seed decays to e⁻¹⁷² and ATR(14)'s to e⁻³²⁰ — both exactly zero in double precision — so anything other than an exact match would have been a finding. `assert_buffer_sufficient` also passes on **all nine folds** for ETHUSDT against a 60-day comparison, so this is not a property of one conveniently-chosen fold.

**The planted mutation: a 5-day buffer IS detected.** Required, because a sufficiency test that cannot detect an insufficient buffer proves nothing.

| indicator | 5d vs 45d | detail |
|---|---|---|
| **Session RVOL baseline** | **DIFFERS** | **1,440 NaN mismatches** on every symbol — exactly 15 days × 96 bars |
| **EMA50** | **DIFFERS** | 118–361 bars differ, `max_rel_diff` ~1.3×10⁻¹¹ to 1.4×10⁻¹⁴ |
| ATR(14) | differs on ETH/SOL, identical on BTC | 2–14 bars, `max_rel` ~1–2×10⁻¹⁶ (1–2 ULP) |
| RSI | differs | 6–12 bars, ~10⁻¹⁶ |
| EMA20 | identical | forgets within 5 days: (1−2/21)⁴⁸⁰ = e⁻⁴⁸ |
| Donchian-20 | identical | finite 20-bar window, long since passed |

The test asserts the specific, predicted symptoms rather than just "something differed": exactly `15 × 96` NaN mismatches in RVOL, and a strictly positive `max_rel_diff` on EMA50. `assert_buffer_sufficient` is separately shown to *raise* when the buffer is short.

**Where the 45 days is actually spent — a finding worth recording.** The binding component is the **20-day RVOL slot baseline**, not EMA50. At a 5-day buffer, RVOL is *structurally absent* for the first 15 days of the traded population, while EMA50's residual is ~10⁻¹¹ relative — numerically detectable but economically irrelevant. That confirms §4.2's own reasoning ("binding component ~30 days: `baseline_days` at sweep maximum…") and locates it precisely. A further test records that **25 days already suffices** for every indicator, so the 45-day buffer carries roughly 20 days of measured headroom rather than assumed headroom.

### The literal guard (§4.2's stated requirement)

`test_no_signal_originates_inside_the_buffer` passes on all three symbols: no signal in the traded population carries a timestamp before `train_start`.

It is included because §4.2 asks for it, **but it is not what establishes sufficiency** — it passes for any buffer length, since slicing to the train window afterwards satisfies it trivially. To stop it being vacuous in the other direction it also asserts that signals *do* occur inside the buffer (they are simply not traded); if that count were zero the guard would be passing because there was nothing to catch.

### Other coverage

Determinism (schedule built twice is identical, and builds with every data-loading path monkeypatched to raise); the rule refuses to be bent (`train_months=12` or `test_months=6` raise rather than silently yielding a different count); `add_months` leap-year and month-length clamping; boundary conventions (00:00 / 23:45 / exclusive-end helper); the tracked artifact matches what the rule produces today.

Zero unlabelled bars in any fold: the regime warm-up (2,974 bars) is entirely consumed in early 2022, well before fold 1's test period begins.

**The whole-period figure conceals a great deal.** m\* concordance ranges **0.159 → 0.661**, a spread of 0.50 — wider than the whole-period value itself. Efficiency ranges 0.189 → 0.530, spread 0.34. So "the three symbols agree about 45% of the time on m\*" is true on average and misleading per fold: in fold 7 they agree two-thirds of the time, in fold 9 barely a sixth.

This matters directly for how §4.4's two-of-three rule is read. That rule assumes BTC/ETH/SOL are partially independent observations, and 4.4 already notes the concordance measurement "moderates interpretation rather than changing the rule". The per-fold view sharpens that: **the degree of independence is itself unstable across time.** A two-of-three result driven by folds 3, 4 and 7 (concordance 0.55–0.66) is weaker evidence than one driven by folds 1, 9 and 6 (0.16–0.26), and the aggregate figure cannot distinguish them. Recorded as description only, with no threshold attached.

The dominant cell also moves with the market in a way that reads sensibly: folds 3–4 (mid-2023) sit in `high|high|high` on m\* — all three symbols squeezed against their cost floors simultaneously — while fold 7 (Q2 2024) sits in `low|low|low`. Fold 1 (Q4 2022) has no cell above 21.5%, which is why its concordance is lowest.

## 6. Judgment calls where the specification was ambiguous

**6.1 — "Bar count in each period" was reported for all three periods, including warm-up.** The brief asked for bar counts per period per symbol without listing which periods. I report warmup/train/test, since the warm-up count (4,320 = 45 × 96 on every fold) is the number that demonstrates the buffer is actually full — which is the point of §4.2.

**6.2 — end-of-range convention.** The repo already contains two conventions: fold boundaries are naturally *inclusive* ("test_end 2024-12-31"), while the existing regime `concordance()` takes an *exclusive* `end_ts`. Mixing them silently drops or double-counts the final bar of a period. I added `day_end_exclusive_ms()` alongside `day_last_bar_ms()` and used the exclusive form for every concordance call, so fold 9's test period includes its 2024-12-31 23:45 bar. Both helpers are tested against each other.

**6.3 — which config to use for the signal-count guard.** `generate_signals` needs a `CostConfig`, which requires the four Point 3R parameters that have no defaults. None of them affects any indicator compared for sufficiency (`rvol_threshold` gates which bars *signal*, not what the baseline *computes*), so they are explicitly-arbitrary scaffolding, and a test asserts indicator values are invariant to them. `baseline_days` is the exception — it genuinely changes the RVOL baseline — so it is held at **20**, the §4.3 fixed value and sweep maximum, rather than varied.

**6.4 — fold 5 chosen for the detailed sufficiency comparison.** Mid-dataset, so neither end of the series is special. The all-folds check (`assert_buffer_sufficient` across all nine) runs on one symbol to keep the suite fast; the three-symbol comparison runs on fold 5. Nothing about the result depends on the choice — the all-folds check would have caught a fold-specific failure.

**6.5 — `folds.json` location.** Placed at `data/derived/folds/folds.json`, mirroring `data/derived/regime/terciles.json`. The brief said "the same directory convention `src/regime/` uses", which I read as *a module-named subdirectory under `data/derived/`*, not literally inside `regime/`.

## 7. Where I think the specification is worth questioning

**7.1 — the 45-day buffer has more headroom than §4.2 implies, and the binding component is more specific.** §4.2 says the binding component is "~30 days: `baseline_days` at sweep maximum, the 30-day regime window, ATR(14), Donchian-20, and EMA50 (which needs ~200 bars to converge to within rounding, not 50)". Measured: **25 days already gives bit-identical values for every strategy indicator**, and the single binding component is the 20-day RVOL slot baseline. ATR(14) and Donchian-20 contribute nothing to the requirement (both forget within 5 days), and EMA50's contribution is ~10⁻¹¹ relative at 5 days, i.e. it has converged far faster than "~200 bars to within rounding" suggests.

Two caveats before anyone shortens the buffer. First, the **30-day regime window is not in this measurement** — regime labels are computed by `src/regime/`, not by the fold indicators, and they need 30 days plus a day for the liquidity sum (2,974 bars ≈ 31 days). If regime labels ever had to be computed *within* a fold rather than read from the frozen artifact, 30+ days would bind and 25 would not suffice. Second, §4.2's stated purpose for the extra margin is "so the number does not move if a sweep range widens slightly", which is a robustness argument rather than a convergence one. **The 45 days is well-justified; it is simply justified by different components than the text names.** No change recommended — the buffer is free.

**7.2 — "no trade may originate from a bar inside the buffer" is the weaker of the two available tests, and §4.2 presents it as the required one.** §4.2 says: *"Required test: no trade may originate from a bar inside the buffer… This is a test, not a hope."* As implemented anywhere that slices to the train window, that test cannot fail. It is a hope in the shape of a test. The sufficiency comparison (45 vs 90 bit-identical, plus a short-buffer mutation that must be detected) is what actually establishes the buffer works. Both are implemented; I would suggest the pre-registration's wording be understood as satisfied by the pair, not by the literal check alone. Flagged rather than changed — §4.2 is frozen and this is an observation about how to read it, not a defect in the design.

**7.3 — per-fold concordance instability is a finding §4.4 should probably absorb.** §4.4 says the concordance measurement "moderates interpretation rather than changing the rule", which was written when only a whole-period number existed. The per-fold spread (0.159–0.661 on m\*) is large enough that *which folds* drive a two-of-three verdict materially changes how much independent evidence it represents. This does not warrant changing the rule — and amending it now, pre-lift, on the basis of a structural measurement would be permissible but is not obviously wise. Recording it so the fold-level figures are on the table when the two-of-three verdict is read.

**7.4 — nothing in §4.2 says what happens if a fold's test period contains a data gap.** All nine folds have complete grids (every cell exactly `days × 96`), so the question is moot here and the code does not handle it. Worth knowing it is unhandled if the dataset is ever rebuilt or extended.
